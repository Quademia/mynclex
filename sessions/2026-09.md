### Part one — the lock, and the half of it that was a habit

Four fields are locked once a product exists: Product ID, Type, duration,
and paid-vs-trial. The stated reason is *"never mutate meaning under sold
rows."*

⭐ **Sam did not accept it, and was right.** His question — *"I thought
what they bought gets captured to the subscription table; making changes
to the product table should affect future buys, is that the situation?"* —
is exactly the situation, and the code proves it harder than the doc
claims. `end_at` is a real date computed once; `cat_allowance` is **copied
onto the subscription** with a comment saying it is frozen *"so a later
catalogue edit never changes what an existing buyer holds"*; the payment
row carries its own amount. Nine reads of `duration_days` exist and every
one is admin or pre-purchase. **No post-purchase surface reads the product
row for a length at all.**

⚠ Which exposes an inconsistency in the rule as written: **price is freely
editable**, and a price is a *stronger* claim about a past sale than a
duration — somebody paid that exact number and it is on their receipt. The
rule locks the weaker claim and leaves the stronger one open. Not a
principle; an accident of which fields felt frightening on the day.

⭐⭐ **Sam's own reason for leaving it locked was better than the doc's,
and better than mine.** Not integrity — naming. `BANK_90D` granting 120
days is a worse artefact than the lock is a nuisance, and the alternative
(tiers named so length is not baked into identity) is a bigger change than
the problem deserves. **Left locked, deliberately, for a reason that is
now written down.**

Verified for the record: **nothing in the database enforces any of this** —
no trigger, no rule; the lock is entirely the form hiding a field and the
save action never reading it. So a direct `UPDATE` of `duration_days`
works, takes effect immediately, and harms nobody. **Product ID is the
exception the other way**: payments and subscriptions reference it, so
Postgres itself refuses to change it. That lock is physics, not policy.
ⓘ And a row update is data, not schema — it does not touch the migration
tracker, so the `db push` trap does not apply to editing a price on prod.

### Part two — the trial email that said "7-day" in words

Zooming in on the trial asked what would happen if its length changed. The
pages all follow the catalogue — the strip, the checkout headings, the
lapsed wall, the picker rail, and `/help` never mentions it. **Three
strings did not:** the subject and both ledes of `payment.received`
hardcoded "7-day". Customer-facing, and the first email a new trialler
ever gets.

⭐ **The obvious fix was the wrong one.** The pages get it right by asking
the catalogue at render time; copying that into the template would have
been worse than the bug, because an email is written once and read
forever — a live read at send time promises 14 days to someone who started
a 7-day trial four minutes earlier. So the number travels **on the order**,
resolved at enqueue from the same product row the grant is computed from:
the same freeze as `end_at` and `cat_allowance`, and the doc's own
§*"The payload is a SNAPSHOT, not a lookup"* with a number instead of a
date.

- Optional field; absent loses the number rather than inventing one
  ("free" stands in), per the standing rule that a new payload field
  defaults to the old behaviour.
- ⚠ Read **outside** the grants block, which skips `SETUP_REQUIRED` — the
  framing with no subscription and no end date, where the length is the
  only thing the email can say about how much time is on offer. The branch
  that needs it most, and the one a careless fix would have missed.
- ⚠⚠ **The trial had no preview fixture at all** — a whole second
  dimension over the three framings, on the busiest door into the product,
  exactly the gap that section of the file warns about. Three added.

### Part three — the badge that was a magic number

changes by any route.

⭐ **Text, not a boolean** — the decision that made the change worth
making. A boolean moves which product wears the badge and leaves the words

claim about what customers choose, which pre-launch nobody can support.
**Same honesty rule already settled for struck-through "was" prices**,

⭐ **Sam widened it twice, correctly both times.** *"I think we should do

- ⓘ **No limit on how many carry one**, on Sam's instruction — *"let's not

- ⚠ The readiness highlight is border + shadow **only**. The bank version

- ⚠ **24 characters, measured rather than invented.** At 375px the cards

⚠ **Screenshots of both public pages came back blank** under the in-app
browser despite the DOM being fully laid out, so verification was done by

### Part two — ⭐ the wall that could not say what ended

Sam asked what an expired trial looks like on login, and guessed *"same
as if any plan expired."* Correct, and stronger than he expected: **it is
also identical to someone who has never bought anything.**

`bankAccessForUser` filters to passes that are still valid, so the row
that explains a refusal is precisely the row it excludes. Three
populations, one `active: false`, one hedged sentence — *"your access may
have expired, or you don't have a pass yet"* — and a picker rail that
went **silent**: "7 days left" one morning, a blank space the next.

⚠ **I was wrong about the mechanism and said so.** I told Sam expiry was
computed only at read time and nothing flipped a status. There **is** a
nightly sweep — step 4c of `nclex_enrolment_nightly_sweep()`, and the
migration comment even records the single dev row it had already fired on
(2026-08-09). It did not change the fix, but it changed the query: a
lapsed row is `ACTIVE`-with-a-past-`end_at` **or** `EXPIRED` depending
only on whether the cron has run, so both are accepted. `REVOKED` is
excluded — a refund is not an expiry.

Built: `BankAccess` gains `reason` (`ACTIVE`/`LAPSED_TRIAL`/`LAPSED_PAID`/
`NEVER`) and `endedAt`, from a second query that runs **only** when the
first finds nothing. Wall gets three true sentences with a date; the rail
keeps a line. Additive — `active` unchanged, no existing caller touched.

⭐ **Sam's ruling:** trial → paid → lapsed reads as `LAPSED_PAID`, since
the paid pass is the access actually lost. Ordered `end_at DESC`.

All four branches proven against real dev rows, including backdating a
paid subscription to manufacture the `LAPSED_PAID` case that did not
otherwise exist (`4ed777d7` had re-bought). Both fixtures restored.

⭐ Sam asked for the lapsed line in red. **Not `var(--danger)`** —
`#dc2626` is the text colour of `.alert-error` on `#fef2f2`, built for
dark-on-pale, and on the rail's navy gradient it falls to roughly 1.6:1
and reads as mud. `#fca5a5`, ~5.9:1, verified by rendering both states
side by side rather than asserting it.

⚠ Two record findings on the way. `nclex_subscriptions_pack_type_check`
restricts the column to `'BANK_DURATION'`, yet `entitlements.ts` filtered
`in ('BANK_DURATION','TRIAL')` and its comment believed `TRIAL` was a
pack type — a value the database **forbids**. Harmless, documented rather
than quietly copied into the new query. And a trial subscription is an
ordinary `BANK_DURATION` row; `source` is its only marker, by design.

⚠ Copy changed beyond the brief and flagged: the never-had-access

Three habits produced the wrong links, and only one was right:

| How | Where | Result |
|---|---|---|
| `headers().get('origin')` | setup / reset links only | ✅ followed the request |
| `NEXT_PUBLIC_SITE_URL ?? prod` | 6 sites in `lib/tutors/actions.ts` | ❌ set **nowhere**, so the fallback always ran |
| a literal in 4 files | incl. `footer.ts` — every email ever sent | ❌ unreachable by any setting |

### ⭐⭐ The design decision, and I was wrong first

I proposed a separate `nclex_trial_requests` table plus its own grant
path, arguing `nclex_payments` records MONEY and a ₵0 row would be a lie
in it. Sam pushed back: *"why am i having a feeling we should write the
0 payment row instead."*

He was right, and the belief I argued from was **false**. Checked
against the constraints: the table already holds `INIT` rows (abandoned,
no money), `FAILED` rows, and `collection_channel='OFF_PLATFORM'` rows
(money that never touched Paystack), and has **no** amount>0 constraint.
It is an **order ledger**, not a cash ledger, so an order priced at zero
is an ordinary member of it.

What his shape bought, all verified rather than assumed:

- **`/welcome` needs no change at all** — it grants by reading
  `nclex_payments`, so a trial order is simply found there.
- **`activateGroup` already branches on whether an account exists**,
  which is exactly the trial's two arrivals (grant now / mint the setup
  link). Nothing re-implements either.
- **Idempotency is inherited** from the subscription's unique
  `payment_id` — the separate table would have had to build it.
- It **deletes the table and the parallel path** the guard was invented
  to justify: the order row IS the record of intent, keyed by email.

⭐ **`BANK_TRIAL` is its own purpose, not `BANK_PURCHASE` at zero** (my
one accepted amendment). A trial recorded as a purchase reads as revenue
in every report that does not check the amount; its own value makes
excluding it a one-word filter and makes trials countable without the
heuristic `amount_minor = 0`. New `collection_channel = 'NONE'`:
nothing was collected *by design*, distinct from `OFF_PLATFORM`, which
asserts money **was** collected, just not by us.

### ⭐ The email: reuse, and Sam was right again

I claimed the trial needed its own template because a receipt reading
*"Total: GHS 0.00"* is the wrong object. Sam: *"its ok to say amount
paid 00 … there is no point trying to be cutesy … its a form of
subscription."*

The template settles it in its own words — `payment-received.ts` carries
a `FRAMING` record with three state-aware entries and a header comment
saying *"adding a sixth purpose later is one entry in PURPOSE_LABEL plus
whatever sentence the enqueue side freezes — not a new email."* And the
grants section already prints **"Bank access until 11 September 2026"**
off the subscription — the single most important sentence in a trial
email, which I had claimed we would have to build.

⭐ **Only the sentences that would be FALSE are overridden**, through a
`TRIAL_OVERLAY` beside `FRAMING` rather than new framing keys: framing
describes the **state** of a grant, trial-ness is a property of the
**order**, and folding two independent dimensions into one enum
multiplies its entries and invites a fourth that means nothing (a trial
can never be `PENDING_APPROVAL`). The amount stays 0.00 — true, and
dressing it up would be worse. The *heading* and the *method* row do not
stay: "Payment received" and "Card" are false, and `isTrial` is optional
so every payload already in the outbox renders as it was queued.

Subject shipped: **"Your free 7-day trial of the question bank (one step
left)"**.

### Built — six slices, one commit

1. Migration `20260924120000` — `BANK_TRIAL` purpose (+ the
   purpose-target CHECK), `collection_channel` `'NONE'`, and the guard.
2. The grant — the `source` ternary in `insertBankSubscriptionOnce`.
3. `lib/payments/trial.ts` — writes the ₵0 order already `PAID` (no
   `INIT`, no reference, no verify) and hands off to
   `activatePendingForEmail`. Plus `trialStateForViewer` for the page.
4. The trial panel on the existing `/checkout/bank` page (Sam's call:
   same route, not a sibling — my `/start-trial` argument was taste).
   `CheckoutShell` gained an optional `submit` override + three copy
   props; the dup-check stays ON for the trial on purpose.
5. The email — two purpose additions and the overlay. No new template,
   no new event key.
6. The front door — the button un-disabled, both CTAs repointed, and
   **every trial mention on `/bank-access` now conditional on an active
   trial product existing**, so the promise can never outrun the app the
   way it just did.

⭐ **The guard is two layers and neither is filtered by status** — one
trial **EVER**, not one *active* trial, or the pass expires, the student
asks again, and the bank is free forever. Layer 1 is unique on
`lower(email)` where `purpose='BANK_TRIAL'` (all a guest has); layer 2
unique on `nclex_subscriptions(user_id)` where
`source='SELF_TRIAL_SIGNUP'` (once an account exists). Both proven to
refuse a duplicate in SQL, bypassing the app — layer 1 case-insensitively.

### ⚠⚠ What went wrong, and what it teaches

**Sam finished `/welcome` and got no bank access.** The order stayed
`SETUP_REQUIRED`, no subscription, and the entire trace was one line on
the dev server: `activatePendingForEmail failed … This payment type is
not handled yet.`

`activate.ts` has **two** purpose dispatches. `PRODUCT_PURPOSES` is the
router that decides whether a payment reaches `grantProductEntitlement`
at all; the `source` ternary I changed sits **inside** the branch that
list gates. `BANK_TRIAL` was absent, so the trial never reached the line
meant to grant it.

⭐⭐ **The lesson is not "add it to both lists" — it is that I trusted a
file's description of itself.** `activate.ts`'s header narrates the
grant as one flow, I read it, and I did not enumerate the purpose
branches against the code. Same shape as the consequence-table rule
already in memory: *a tidy summary reads complete and isn't.*

⚠ **And nothing could have caught it mechanically.** `PRODUCT_PURPOSES`
was a plain `string[]`, so widening `PaymentPurpose` raised no error —
the repo's own *"the compiler is not a reviewer"* trap, now extended: an
untyped **routing list** is as silent as an untyped column name.
`PaymentRow.purpose` and the list are now typed to the union.

⚠ **A second, worse defect was hiding behind the first.** `/welcome`
calls activation **once** and its link is single-use, so an order that
fails to grant there is stranded with no route back — for any purpose,
not just trials. `startTrial`'s prior-order branch now re-runs
activation and **asks the database whether it granted** rather than
assuming, which is both the correct answer for a signed-in caller and
the only retry path that exists.

⭐ Verified after the fix by driving the whole flow myself — reading the
setup link out of `nclex_email_outbox` rather than an inbox: trial →
email → `/welcome` → password → payment `ACTIVATED`, subscription
`SELF_TRIAL_SIGNUP` / `BANK_DURATION` / cat_allowance 1 / 2026-09-04 →
2026-09-11, and the dashboard reading **"BANK 7-DAY · 7 days left"**.

### The session's one idea

⭐⭐ **The industry gates on the money. We cannot — and that is exactly
why seats exist.**

Thinkific, Teachable, Podia and Kajabi all offer *unlimited students*
because they take a cut of every sale: the more students a creator
enrols, the more those platforms earn, automatically. Their cheap tiers
are not generosity, they are a revenue share. Teachable takes up to
**7.5%**; Thinkific's surcharge steps **5% → 2% → 1% → 0.5% → 0%** as
you climb.

With tutor fees off-platform (§6) **we earn nothing when a tutor
grows.** With no transaction fee *and* no cap, a tutor with 500 students
would pay exactly what a tutor with 5 pays. **Seats are our stand-in for
the transaction fee** — the only lever tying our revenue to a tutor's
success while the money moves outside us.

And it settles what the top of the ladder is for: **the on-platform
capability is where we finally get the industry-standard lever**, so its
advantage is not "a bigger band".

⭐ Seats won because **a trial is a *clock* and this model's unit is a

⭐⭐ And it dissolves §4's ugliest unanswered question — *the trial ends

### ⭐ Why no fourth rung between 50 and 200

The gap is real — a tutor at 65 students would buy a band they will not
fill for two years, which is the standard criticism of seat pricing
(*it penalises growth*). But an extra rung and **buyable overage** solve
the same problem, and overage solves it better: continuous rather than
lumpy, an upsell rather than a decision, no extra column on the pricing
page, and precedented (TalentLMS runs exactly this shape).

> **Overage is the fourth tier, priced continuously.**

⚠ One rule makes it work: **an overage seat must cost more per head than
an in-band seat**, or the cheapest path is Starter plus 190 extra seats
and nobody upgrades.

*notification* is the right control rather than a platform rule about
when a tutor may remove someone: **removal is theirs to decide,
disclosure is ours to guarantee.**

⭐ It also re-justified the 24-month cap, whose original reason
(bounding **our** liability) was deleted four days earlier: **seats are
a stock and must come back** — an unset window consumes one forever, so
without a maximum "seats" is one-way consumption, not a band — and
**nobody can underwrite a decade** of a platform, a programme or a
tutor. Sam's 08-27 principle inverted: *a tutor cannot promise longer
than anyone can underwrite.*

### The access window — required, and NOT pre-filled

⚠ **A refinement of 08-27, not a restatement.** That day settled
*required **and pre-filled***, reasoning that a visible default carries
no hidden convention. **The seat model changed the answer**: a visible
default is still a default, and accepting one unthinkingly now costs the
tutor a seat for up to two years. Under the old liability framing it
cost them nothing.

⚠ Sam's own example also killed *pre-filling proportionately*: **a
four-week self-paced programme may legitimately need a year of access,
depending on the content.** No rule can infer the window from the
programme's length — only the tutor knows, which is exactly why the
responsibility is theirs. ⏭ **The helper text is now the whole
mechanism** and is open.

**No backfill** (Sam: *"they are not real users"*) — so `NOT NULL` is a
one-line default rather than a migration with a policy attached. ⚠ Only
true while it stays true.

### What was researched, and what it cost to check

Comparable platforms were read rather than assumed. ⚠ **Two findings cut
AGAINST our own free tier and are recorded because they are
inconvenient:** MoodleCloud — whose seat shape we borrowed — and
Thinkific **both abolished their permanent free plans in 2026**; and
Zummit Africa ran at **80–90% intake while free and fell to 30%** on
introducing a subscription.

⭐ **Why we proceed anyway, with a test rather than a hope:** none of
those platforms has our second business. A Starter tutor's ten students
are ten warm **Bank** prospects, and Bank revenue is ours outright with
no split. **Starter is acquisition spend, and the standing test is
measurable: are Starter tutors producing Bank subscribers?**
