**PROPOSAL on every price**; what changed is that the *shape* is now
largely settled.

Continues the same day's earlier session (logged in `sessions/2026-08.md`
under 2026-08-27, part two), which had reversed the accountability rule
and turned the enrolment gate into **seats**. This one built the ladder
on top of that.

### The session's one idea

⭐⭐ **The industry gates on the money. We cannot — and that is exactly
why seats exist.**

Thinkific, Teachable, Podia and Kajabi all offer *unlimited students*
because they take a cut of every sale: the more students a creator
enrols, the more those platforms earn, automatically. Their cheap tiers
are not generosity, they are a revenue share. Teachable takes up to
**7.5%**; Thinkific's surcharge steps **5% → 2% → 1% → 0.5% → 0%** as
you climb.

With tutor fees off-platform (§6) **we earn nothing when a tutor
grows.** With no transaction fee *and* no cap, a tutor with 500 students
would pay exactly what a tutor with 5 pays. **Seats are our stand-in for
the transaction fee** — the only lever tying our revenue to a tutor's
success while the money moves outside us.

And it settles what the top of the ladder is for: **the on-platform
capability is where we finally get the industry-standard lever**, so its
advantage is not "a bigger band".

### ⭐ The trial died because the model's unit changed

Sam, on being told Starter would hold ten real seats: *"then there will
be no trial on pro."* Correct, and it is one line:

> **Ten free seats and a 30-day trial exist for the same reason — to let
> a tutor experience the teaching loop, which §4 itself calls the only
> thing that converts. Having both pays twice for one outcome.**

⭐ Seats won because **a trial is a *clock* and this model's unit is a
*stock*.** When the gate was a boolean, a clock was the only way to
grant a temporary yes; since seats, the natural way to grant a taste is
free *seats*, not free *days*.

⭐⭐ And it dissolves §4's ugliest unanswered question — *the trial ends
and the tutor has 8 live students, now what?* With seats there is no
such moment. §4 is **struck through, not deleted**, because its own
reasoning is what killed it.

### ⭐ Why no fourth rung between 50 and 200

The gap is real — a tutor at 65 students would buy a band they will not
fill for two years, which is the standard criticism of seat pricing
(*it penalises growth*). But an extra rung and **buyable overage** solve
the same problem, and overage solves it better: continuous rather than
lumpy, an upsell rather than a decision, no extra column on the pricing
page, and precedented (TalentLMS runs exactly this shape).

> **Overage is the fourth tier, priced continuously.**

⚠ One rule makes it work: **an overage seat must cost more per head than
an in-band seat**, or the cheapest path is Starter plus 190 extra seats
and nobody upgrades.

### ⭐⭐ The capability-vs-rung argument, and Sam changing his mind twice — correctly both times

**First he proposed reintroducing *Partner* as an exclusive top rung**,
reasoning that reaching it proves a serious tutor. **Two things in that
were right and are recorded**: reaching a tier is *continuously
re-earned* evidence where an approval is a snapshot that goes stale; and
a capability you *might* be granted does not sell an upgrade.

**Rejected on four counts**, the sharpest being that it collided with
Sam's own best argument from ten minutes earlier — *"fresh tutors may
not have any form of payments system"* — since a rung at 200 students
serves only tutors who solved payments years ago. Also: tier position
filters for **size**, and size correlates with **more** chargeback
exposure, not less; the forced band jump returns; and it would delay the
transaction-fee business by years, since nobody is at Partner scale in
year one.

⭐ **The asymmetry that decided it:** a rung can be added any time,
whereas the capability cannot be retrofitted to tutors who already left.
So **Partner is deferred with a trigger, not rejected** — three or more
tutors holding the capability and pressing against Academy's band. ⓘ
Deliberately *not* maintained as a live "Plan B": two live plans are two
things to keep true, and they drift.

**Then Sam ruled Starter out of it entirely** — *"i wont allow on
platform payment for starter"* — which was **better than the version I
had recommended**:

- ⭐⭐ It **restores the property that makes the seat cap safe**: only the
  tutor hits the wall, never a student who has already paid.
  On-platform checkout brings the student back as the actor, and that
  would have been worst on Starter — the tightest wall (10) with the
  least-known tutor.
- ⭐ It gives **Starter → Pro a second reason to exist**. The ladder was
  thin; the trigger is now *"more than 10 students"* **or** *"tired of
  chasing 40 MoMo payments"*. In a market of small cohorts that is worth
  more than the free tier is.
- It **deletes a subsystem** — payout holds and graduated approval for
  anonymous free accounts need not exist.

### ⚠⚠ My own error, corrected in the doc rather than quietly amended

**I marked *"Currency — GHS"* and *"Billing interval — annual"* as ✅
SETTLED.** Sam never decided that; I recommended it and then wrote the
recommendation up as a decision. He caught it: *"so far you have assumed
everything will be GHS and MoMo, but we can do both… ideally dollars, so
it will be international."*

⭐ **The correction produced a better answer than the error had.** The
constraint is **asymmetric**, so currency is not a platform question at
all — **it follows the tutor**: a Ghanaian tutor cannot reliably pay in
USD (local cards commonly carry a **$0 international limit**, and banks
restrict forex outflows), while an international tutor has no such
problem. ⓘ The app already renders dual currency (minor units through
`formatMinor()`, dual-priced products), so "both" is an existing
pattern, not new machinery.

**What genuinely survives** is narrower and channel-shaped: ⚠⚠ **MoMo
cannot do recurring at all** (Paystack's own docs). That is a property
of the **channel**, true in any currency — so annual prepaid is forced
on the **MoMo path specifically**, not platform-wide. A card can hold a
real subscription in either currency.

⚠ Recorded as a visible correction because *a recommendation promoted to
a decision* is precisely the failure this document keeps catching in
other people's work — including the ChatGPT memo's 100× price misread
four days earlier.

### ⚠⚠ A LIVE DEFECT found while checking the pause emails — recorded, NOT fixed

Sam asked that a paused or unenrolled student be *"reliably informed by
email… this protects us."* Checking the code first rather than
designing from assumption produced two findings:

**① All three transitions are silent today.** `pauseEnrolmentAction`,
`resumeEnrolmentAction` and `cancelEnrolmentAction` all exist, call
their RPCs and **send nothing** — while `rejectEnrolmentAction`, in the
same file, does send. There is no `enrolment.paused` / `.resumed` /
`.cancelled` trigger in the registry. **A tutor can remove a student's
access today and the student finds out by trying to log in.**

**② ⚠⚠ And the student is told the wrong reason.** There are **two**
pause reasons — `INSTALLMENT_OVERDUE` and `TUTOR_MANUAL` — and one
student-facing sentence in `ENROLMENT_LOCKED_REASON`:

> *"Access paused — a payment is overdue. Your tutor can restore it."*

**A student paused manually by their tutor is told they defaulted when
they did not. It is on prod now.** `paused_reason` is already carried on
the roster row, so only the copy fails to branch. ⓘ **A bug fix, not a
design decision** — left open deliberately when the emails were
deferred, so it does not disappear along with them.

### ⭐ Why the notification became structural rather than a courtesy

> Under a boolean gate a tutor had **no reason** to unenrol anybody.
> **Under seats, every removal is worth money.** And because freeing a
> seat and ending access are the *same act*, the incentive points
> straight at a real student losing real access.

So it is §5's **fourth condition** — the first three all describe the
tutor *lapsing*; this one describes the tutor *acting*. The tutor cannot
suppress it, resume needs its own email, and ⚠⚠ **the registry's
occurrence rule will bite**: a student can be paused → resumed → paused,
and a `stage` keyed on the person means the second email **silently
never sends**.

### ⭐ Sam's framing that settled what §5 is about

> *"This is about access to the programme, not the platform. We are not
> charging any student for access to our platform, our emails or our
> infrastructure. So this really is between the tutor and the student."*

A student has **two independent relationships** — the programme, bought
from their tutor, and the Bank, bought from us. ⭐ Which is why a
*notification* is the right control rather than a platform rule about
when a tutor may remove someone: **removal is theirs to decide,
disclosure is ours to guarantee.**

⭐ It also re-justified the 24-month cap, whose original reason
(bounding **our** liability) was deleted four days earlier: **seats are
a stock and must come back** — an unset window consumes one forever, so
without a maximum "seats" is one-way consumption, not a band — and
**nobody can underwrite a decade** of a platform, a programme or a
tutor. Sam's 08-27 principle inverted: *a tutor cannot promise longer
than anyone can underwrite.*

### The access window — required, and NOT pre-filled

⚠ **A refinement of 08-27, not a restatement.** That day settled
*required **and pre-filled***, reasoning that a visible default carries
no hidden convention. **The seat model changed the answer**: a visible
default is still a default, and accepting one unthinkingly now costs the
tutor a seat for up to two years. Under the old liability framing it
cost them nothing.

⚠ Sam's own example also killed *pre-filling proportionately*: **a
four-week self-paced programme may legitimately need a year of access,
depending on the content.** No rule can infer the window from the
programme's length — only the tutor knows, which is exactly why the
responsibility is theirs. ⏭ **The helper text is now the whole
mechanism** and is open.

**No backfill** (Sam: *"they are not real users"*) — so `NOT NULL` is a
one-line default rather than a migration with a policy attached. ⚠ Only
true while it stays true.

### What was researched, and what it cost to check

Comparable platforms were read rather than assumed. ⚠ **Two findings cut
AGAINST our own free tier and are recorded because they are
inconvenient:** MoodleCloud — whose seat shape we borrowed — and

no split. **Starter is acquisition spend, and the standing test is
measurable: are Starter tutors producing Bank subscribers?**
