### ⭐⭐ Sam reversed the design, and the plan had it backwards for four days

The doc had said since 2026-08-05 that the slice "carries first-time-Google

Sam's question killed it in one sentence: **our platform needs a subscription

⚠ **My first answer to that was wrong and is worth recording.** I argued
against sign-in-only on the grounds that it would mean letting Supabase
create the account and then deleting it — manufacturing the
half-built-account trap on every refusal, with permanent lockout if a delete
ever failed. That reasoning was sound but the premise was not: the
`before-user-created` hook refuses **before** the row is written, so there is
nothing to delete. Sam's instinct was right and the platform supports it
properly. Checked, retracted, and the doc records the retraction rather than
quietly swapping its conclusion.

### The evidence that settled it — and it was not an opinion

Sam pushed back a second time: *"whatever we have built works; all we are
doing is adding a sign-in method — why does a hook come into it?"* Fair, and
it forced the real check. Both account-creating paths look the person up in
`nclex_users` (the **profile** table) and create in `auth.users`:

- `lib/enrolments/actions.ts` — tutor enrols a student
- `lib/payments/activate.ts` — pay-first guest activation

A Google stranger leaves a profile-less row, so both conclude "new person",
both call `inviteUserByEmail`, and both collide with `auth.users`' unique
email index (`users_email_partial_key`). The tutor cannot enrol her — and the
payment path returns *"we could not send the setup email"*, meaning **she has
paid and cannot get in**. `/register` refuses her too ("already registered",
a message `app/register/actions.ts` already knows by name). She would never
learn why: she clicked a button weeks earlier and was told "no account".

### ⭐ The hook looks nothing up, which is the whole elegance

Supabase links a new OAuth identity to an existing user automatically, so a
**creation** arriving via Google is itself proof that no account exists. The
rule needs no query: `provider = 'google'` → refuse. Supabase's own worked
example is provider-based rejection, nearly this case exactly.

It also **cannot shut the other doors** — `/register` and both invite paths
arrive as provider `email` and fall through. The narrowness is structural,
not a condition we hand-wrote carefully and hoped was right. That dissolved
the sharpest edge I had flagged twice.

### ⭐ Sam's naming call: retire, don't repurpose

`GOOGLE_FIRST_SIGNIN` had been pre-loaded into the constraint since 2a. Under
sign-in-only there is no first sign-in that creates an account, so the name
would have survived describing something it no longer meant. Sam said rename
it. I pushed one step further — a lone `GOOGLE_SIGNIN` would repeat the arc's
own mistake for the **fourth** time (a success type with no refusal partner;
the last two cost a migration each), so it shipped as the pair every other
door already has: **`GOOGLE_LOGIN_OK` + `GOOGLE_BLOCKED`**. Free to retire —
zero rows on dev and prod, both checked first.

Postgres **ORs** the SELECT policies on a table. `nclex_programmes`
carries two — `nclex_programmes_student_select`

enrolment filter in its query body**; its own header comment said RLS

is both, who then sees every programme they authored in the STUDENT
list.

**Which is why clearing his enrolments could never have fixed it** — and
I had twice tried to fix the symptom before he named the cause. The
lesson worth keeping: **RLS is a security boundary, not a view filter.**
A surface that means "things I am enrolled in" has to say so itself.
Fixed in `lib/programmes/student-actions.ts`, two lines. Listing only —
`require-programme-access` always gated entry, so it was never an access
hole. vitest 855/855; tsc at the two known pre-existing errors.

### Three traps that cost real time

**1. `nclex_programmes_seed_units_trg` fights deterministic ids.** It

**2. Attendance does not follow the enrolment.**
