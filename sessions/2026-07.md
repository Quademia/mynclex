### It began as an investigation, not a build

Sam asked for a report on the quiz **modes** — how intent and mode relate,
and what the runner actually does with them. Reading the plan against the
code produced **four findings**, and they shaped everything after.

**The mode framework lives in three documents, and the one meant to unify
them was never written.** `bank-consumption.html` §15 has the framework,
attempt-creation §6.1.2 the intent×mode tuple, runner §8/§10.1/§15 the
timer, submit model and navigation. Runner **§4 — "Per-mode behaviour
matrix"** lists exactly the axes Sam asked about and its entire body reads
*"To be filled in."* That empty section is the root cause of the drift
below.

**Finding 1 — the engagement clock does not exist.** The builder tells
STUDY students *"Engagement-clock — pauses if you step away"*, and §6.1.2
says STUDY uses an engagement-clock where EXAM uses a wall-clock. But
runner §8 — **settled later, 2026-05-09** — defines the countdown as
running toward `started_at + duration_seconds` with **no intent branch**,
and the code matches §8: `elapsedSec = (nowMs - startedAtMs) / 1000`. No
pause logic; no pause columns on `nclex_attempts`. A STUDY timed attempt
burns its clock while the student is away. The word "engagement" does have
a real implementation — the per-question time engine — but §8 explicitly
says that is a different clock that does not sum with this one.

**Finding 2 — STUDY-timed and EXAM-timed are mechanically identical.**
Same duration (90s × count, from a trigger branching on **mode only**),
same wall clock, same nav, feedback and submit model. Label and copy are
the only difference.

**Finding 3 — resume is a three-way contradiction.** Parent §15: "impossible
for any timed mode". §6.1.2: rewrites to "STUDY intent only". The code
(slice 4.5a): resumes **everything except CAT** — `mode ≠ 'CAT'`, no intent
condition, at either call site. Meanwhile the Builder tells EXAM students
*"single sitting · cannot be resumed"* and an abandoned EXAM attempt **does**
reappear in the Resume banner.

**Finding 4 — `intent` drives no behaviour at all.** Across the entire
session route it is read in **exactly one place** — `preflight.tsx`, to
print the word "Study" or "Exam". Every mechanical difference comes from
`mode` + `duration_seconds`.

### The two cuts — and the mirror nobody would have looked for

Sam's read was that some modes conflict. The investigation supported it more
sharply than "conflicting": **each removed tuple was mechanically identical
to its twin under the other intent**, so each was a duplicate card
distinguished only by a label making a promise the runner does not keep.

- **(EXAM, Untimed Test)** — untimed means `duration_seconds = NULL`, so
  there was no clock either way. "Exam framing without time pressure" is
  precisely what the STUDY version already is.
- **(STUDY, Timed Sequential)** — forward-only reproduces exam mechanics
  (NCSBN: answer in order, no going back, no skipping) and teaches nothing
  on its own. An exam constraint wearing a study label.

**The blast radius was bigger than the student builder.** `nclex_tutor_quizzes`
has **no intent column** — intent is DERIVED at launch (MOCK→EXAM,
PRACTICE→STUDY) — so its `kind_mode_tuple` CHECK is a mirror of the attempts
one. Left alone, a tutor could author a quiz that exploded on launch against
the tightened constraint. MOCK lost Untimed Test, PRACTICE lost Timed
Sequential: **7 pairs → 5**. Verified first that no existing quiz used
either pairing; the migration **refuses to run** if one turns up rather than
silently rewriting a tutor's authored mode.

**Three dev attempt rows converted, not deleted** (Sam's call — one is a
COMPLETED attempt, and deleting it would punch a hole in a student's history
for a naming decision they never made). This is also **load-bearing for
display**: four read paths resolve a mode's label via
`(intent === 'STUDY' ? MODES_STUDY : MODES_EXAM).find(...)` and fall back to
the raw id, so a row left on a removed tuple would print `UNTIMED_TEST` in
History. Order matters — the conversion runs **before** the constraint
rebuild, since adding a CHECK validates against existing rows.

**Verified on dev, not assumed:** conversions landed exactly as predicted
(STUDY/Untimed Test 3→5, STUDY/Timed Free Nav 0→1, both removed tuples at
zero); constraint definitions read 6 and 5; and **both removed tuples were
proven actively REJECTED** by a live rollback-only UPDATE rather than read
off the definition. In-browser: Study 3 modes, Exam 3, tutor form 3 for
PRACTICE and 2 for MOCK.

Landing shape — **STUDY**: Untimed Learning · Untimed Test · Timed Free Nav.
**EXAM**: Timed Free Nav · Timed Sequential · CAT.

### The naming arc — and the four competing vocabularies under it

Sam opened the naming question. The structural observation that unlocked it:
**"Untimed Learning" and "Untimed Test" are named after the axis they SHARE,
not the one that separates them** — both lead with the clock, when the real
difference is *when the truth arrives*. The names point at the wrong
variable, which is why the structure was hard to see.

A **web search** grounded the options rather than inventing them: AMBOSS
ships "Study Mode"/"Exam Mode" as its two frames (validating our *intent*
names, and ruling those words out one level down); **"Tutor" is the
NCLEX-native term** for immediate rationale across UWorld, Archer and
Bootcamp; UWorld's "Untimed" means exactly our Untimed Test; and NCSBN's own
rules make forward-only the *authentic* mode, not an arbitrary one. Recorded
as `bank-consumption.html` **§15.1 — PROPOSED, NOT APPLIED**, together with
the reasoning for **rejecting numbered mode names** ("Study Mode 1/2/3" — the
number becomes the headline while meaning drops to subtitle; position-based
numbers break exactly when the list changes, as it just did; higher numbers
imply a ranking that isn't real) and a **naming avoid-list**
(Practice/Mock are tutor quiz *kinds*; Study/Exam are the *intent* names).

Sam then set **interim** labels — STUDY: Learning · Untimed practice · Timed
practice; EXAM: Free Navigation · Sequential · Computer Adaptive Testing
(CAT). The shape has its own logic: one "Learning" mode plus two "practice"
modes separated only by the clock, which is their only real difference — and
"Free Nav" could be dropped from Sequential's sibling precisely *because*
Timed Sequential had just left STUDY, leaving nothing to contrast against.
"Exams" was dropped from the EXAM pair after it read twice over on the
surfaces that prefix with the intent themselves ("Start Exam · Sequential
Exams").

**⚠ The rename exposed FOUR competing label lists.** `filter-config`'s two
arrays, plus one each hardcoded in `runner.tsx` and `preflight.tsx` **keyed
on mode alone**, plus a fourth in `lib/tutor-quiz/format.ts`. The two
mode-keyed ones cannot express a per-intent name, so renaming would have left
the runner topbar and preflight still saying "Untimed Test" for the same
attempt the builder called "Untimed practice" — and they had already drifted
into **three spellings of one mode** ("Timed · Free Nav" / "Timed · free
navigation" / "Timed · free nav"). All four now resolve through one
`modeLabelFor(intent, mode)`.

The tutor side is the sharpest case: `formatQuizMode` now **requires** the
quiz kind, because TIMED_FREE_NAV is the one mode allowed under both and is
named differently under each. **Proven on real rows** — the tutor quiz list
renders the *same mode id* as "Timed practice" for the PRACTICE quiz and
"Free Navigation" for the MOCK. Nine call sites updated; every one already
carried `quiz_kind`. A tutor was previously configuring quizzes in words no
student ever reads.

The public `/bank-access` demo was resynced and **stays hardcoded** (Sam's
call — it must render for a signed-out visitor with no data behind it); the
file now names the source of truth and the cost of that choice, since it had
already drifted a full naming generation.

### Six things the prototype said that our data doesn't

Checked before building, against the live schema and real rows rather than
against the design's story:

1. **⚠ The readiness pack has no pass/fail.** The card showed a green
   "Passed" pill and 2c drew a pass tick at 65%. Our packs return one of
   **four band words** (Building / Approaching / **Ready 75+** / Excelling).
   "Passed" appears nowhere in the product, and 65 is not our number.
2. **⚠ There is no "At standard" for a CAT.** `CatVerdict` is binary. The
   three-step meter invented a middle rung that can never light.
3. **⚠⚠ The design could punish a student for what she hadn't bought.**
   Two of the four signals are separate purchases, and ○ meant both "you
   fell short" and "you never attempted this". A bank-only student with
   2,000 answers at 78% would read *"Not yet ready — 1 of 4 signals met"*,
   with no route to the top band that doesn't involve spending more money.
   **This was the finding that mattered most.**
4. **The 1,500 target was invented** — the same class of thing as the old
   68% ring, smaller. Sam reframed it as an *evidence threshold*, which
   changed what it is rather than papering over it (see below).
5. **A red 55% pill on a practice quiz** — a practice set has no pass mark,
   so red invents a verdict.
6. **The CAT mini-chart didn't read at 120px** — a green smudge with the
   dashed passing line, the entire point of the chart, invisible.

Plus two reverts of decisions already settled in the cards 1–7 walk: the
resume banner and recent rows had regained an invented subject
("Pharmacology · Untimed practice"), which a filter-built quiz cannot have.

### The four decisions that replaced the design's readiness card

Settled with Sam, then built into one pure file
(`lib/home/student/bank/readiness.ts`, 27 tests) so none of them can erode
into the view:

1. **Three signal states** — met / not met / **not yet attempted**. The band
   counts only what was **attempted**, and an unattempted signal renders as
   a doorway ("Sit a pack →"), never as a mark against the student.
2. **The band reuses the packs' vocabulary** — Building / Approaching /
   Ready. Without this the page carried *three* vocabularies for one idea.
   **Excelling excluded** (a pack earns it with a real score out of 100;
   three yes/no signals can't claim it), and **Ready needs ≥2 attempted
   signals** — clearing accuracy alone is real but thin ground for telling
   someone they're ready for an exam.
3. **Volume is the GATE, not a signal.** Results say how well you did;
   volume says how much we know. As a fourth signal, 1,600 answers at 45%
   would tick a box and read as progress. Below the gate the band is
   withheld — **which also supplies the first-week empty state the handoff
   README explicitly left undesigned**.
4. **⚠ The copy never promises odds.** Sam's framing was "at about 1,500
   answers we could do an estimate of your chances" — but a real pass
   probability needs outcome data from nurses who have sat the NCLEX. The
   threshold buys **reliability** and says so; a test asserts the words
   "chance" and "probability" never appear.

**The accuracy bar is 75, not the design's 65** — 75 is the boundary our own
packs call Ready, the one number documented as pass-associated. A lower
figure would put two bars on "good enough" and only one would trace to
anything. ⬜ Open whether it's too strict; it's one constant.

### A level correction the plan had wrong

§19.4's walk called the mastery bars "the 8 **L1 category** bars". There are
only **four** L1 client-needs categories; the **eight** the design draws are
the **L2 subcategories**. Bars and nudge therefore work at L2 — which is
also the level `weakestSubcategory()` and the builder's `?subcat=` deep link
already speak, so **"Drill it" reuses the link the readiness report already
uses** rather than inventing one.

### The streak — shared, then deliberately un-shared

Stage 1 extracted the programme home's streak maths into a shared pure
module. **Sam reversed it**: a programme streak and a bank streak measure
different achievements from different tables and should be free to define a
"study day" differently — **which they immediately do**. The bank's now
requires an **answered question** (SUBMITTED / AUTO_SUBMITTED), not merely
an attempt row: a streak you can hold by building a quiz, looking at
question 1 and closing the tab isn't measuring study. Verified against SQL
before looking at the page — the strict rule removes exactly two days on the
test account (20 July, 28 June), both days an attempt was opened and nothing
answered, taking the longest run 6 → 5. The page then showed exactly that.
**CAT counts toward the streak** (it is study) but never toward **mastery**
(§13.2). `submitted_at` is coalesced to `updated_at`/`created_at` — ~10
SUBMITTED rows carry none, and filtering on it alone would punch silent
holes in the streak.

### 1. A timed-out CAT now gets a verdict — `2088352`

branch is reached because an answer just landed, and back-dating there would

### What was built — Slice 7, the CAT results page (§13) — `7462682`

The surface a student lands on when a CAT ends, and re-opens from the history
list. One page, both contexts. All seven §13 regions in the plan's priority
order: verdict → readiness probability → items administered → trajectory graph
→ Client Needs breakdown → clinical judgment → compare-to-previous → CTAs. The
abandoned CAT is a **separate surface**, not the report with its regions

clinical-judgment read from the stored case answers.

### Two decisions reopened — noted, nothing changed in code

**§9.3 — the 4-hour time limit** (`beeea85`). Sam: real NCLEX gives 5. The 4 was
deliberate, but three things argue against it — it contradicts §9.2's "match real
NCLEX exactly" principle applied one section earlier; a time limit is a *ceiling*,
so shortening it can only cut off a slow student, making the "less punishing"
rationale inverted; and the first real CAT's pace (1.4 min/item) puts a 150-item
run at ~3h35m, close enough that a 2-min/item student times out near Q120.
Against a straight 5: real NCLEX's 5 hours includes tutorial and breaks, so 5
hours of pure answering is *more* generous than the real exam. Open: 5h / 4.5h /
keep 4h.

**§9.1.1 — the passing standard** (`7976528`). Raised by Sam on seeing the report.
The finding that opens it: **the standard was never independently chosen.** Three
things in the engine are zero for unrelated reasons — `PRIOR_MEAN`, the Medium
difficulty rung, and `PASSING_THETA`. The first two are principled; the third
inherited zero because zero was already the centre of a scale we invented. So
"Above standard" means "better than the middle of our own difficulty labelling."
Sam's argument: for an exam this consequential, passing should not sit at the
midpoint by construction. Not cosmetic — the real run cleared 0.0 by **0.025**
(band [0.025, 0.935]), so a bar even 0.1 higher means that exam does not stop at
85. Probabilities at candidate bars recorded (0.0 → 98.1%, 0.25 → 83.9%, 0.5 →
46.5%, 0.75 → 12.2%). **⚠ Recorded trap:** a 0.5 bar would dissolve §7.7's
saturation — a consequence, *not* a reason. Sam's root-cause read, recorded as
his: the engine works, the **three-rung ladder** is the problem, so widen the
spread first; setting a precise bar on a blunt scale is false precision. Route
out is **standard setting** (NCSBN uses expert panels, not pass statistics), so
outcome data is not the only path. ⚠ Unlike §9.3, changing this **re-interprets
every CAT already sat** — verdicts are computed live from stored theta — so
decide whether history is re-read or pinned.

  have no student-write RLS). Idempotent + race-safe by its WHERE clause
  (re-checks activated / not-terminal / deadline-past, satisfying the
  expiry-requires-activation + one-ending CHECKs); best-effort (returns 0 on
  error, caller carries on).
- `lib/payments/readiness-packs-view.ts` — sweeps before the read (self-heals
  the row) **and** uses `isLapsedLive` in the stage derivation so the card flips
  to lapsed/claimable even in the rare render where the best-effort write didn't
  land. One clock read (`now`) reused throughout.

**Design — the 4 open questions settled one-at-a-time with Sam** (BUILD_LIST +

- **Q1 — can a row have zero correct?** No — **require ≥1 correct per row.**

  (paywalled pages 403'd, but several independent sources converge): Matrix MR
  = 4–10 rows, 2–3 cols, per-column +/- scoring floored at 0, and crucially the
  real exam **forces the student to tick ≥1 box per row to proceed** — which
  makes an all-false row literally unanswerable. So real items always have ≥1
  correct per row. Surfaced as a clear editor block, not a silent fail.
- **Q2 — scoring:** **SATA-style per row.** New `scorePerRowMulti` in
  `lib/scoring/functions.ts` runs the existing `scorePlusMinus` per row (+1
  correct / −1 wrong, floored at 0 within the row), summed across rows. Max
  marks = total correct cells across all rows (a row with 2/1/3 correct → 6
  marks). `is_correct` (full credit) only when every row is exactly right.
- **Q3 — submit gate:** **hard gate** — the student must pick ≥1 per row; the
  runner's submit hint **names the still-empty rows** ("row 2, 4 still empty").
- **Q4 — bounds:** mirror MATRIX exactly (hard **2–10 rows / 2–6 cols**) + an
  advise > block amber advisory toward the NGN norm (4–10 rows, 2–3 cols).
